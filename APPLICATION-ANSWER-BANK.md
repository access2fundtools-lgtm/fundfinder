# PART 2 — FUNDFINDER

## One line (under 20 words)

> FundFinder AI finds grants, loans and accelerators open to Nigerian businesses, and
> matches each founder to the ones they actually qualify for.

## Short description (50 words)

> FundFinder AI is a funding intelligence platform for Nigerian SMEs. It scans hundreds
> of sources daily, verifies live grants, loans and accelerator programmes, and matches
> each opportunity to a business profile. Founders see only what they qualify for, and
> get AI help drafting the application. Free to use.

## The problem (100 words)

> Nigeria has over 40 million MSMEs and the single biggest barrier they name is access to
> finance. The money exists — government schemes, development finance, bank products,
> foundation grants, accelerators — but it is scattered across hundreds of websites, PDFs
> and social media posts with no central, reliable source.
>
> Three things follow. Founders never hear about opportunities they qualify for.
> Deadlines pass unnoticed. And when they do find something, the application process
> assumes a level of documentation and financial literacy most small businesses do not
> have. The capital is not missing. The route to it is.

## The solution (100 words)

> FundFinder closes the gap in three steps.
>
> **Discover.** An automated engine scans hundreds of sources and publishes verified,
> live opportunities daily. Nothing is stale, because the pipeline refreshes itself.
>
> **Match.** Each founder builds a business profile once — sector, stage, location, size.
> Our matching engine scores every opportunity against it, so a twenty-item list becomes
> a three-item shortlist of things they can actually win.
>
> **Apply.** AI drafts the application, checks eligibility and lists required documents.
> Nothing is ever submitted without the founder reviewing it first.

## Target market

> Primary: Nigeria's 40 million MSMEs, focused on registered businesses seeking their
> first or next external funding. Early concentration in agribusiness, trade, retail and
> technology, and among women-led and youth-led enterprises, which are over-represented
> in grant eligibility criteria and under-represented in successful applications.
>
> Secondary: grant-makers, DFIs and government agencies who need qualified, pre-screened
> applicants, and currently pay agencies for reach they cannot verify.

## Business model

> Free discovery drives reach. Revenue comes from three places:
>
> 1. **Credits** — founders buy credits for AI-drafted applications and premium matching.
> 2. **Subscription** — monthly access to premium matching and priority alerts.
> 3. **Programme partners** — grant-makers and DFIs pay to publish programmes and receive
>    pre-screened, eligibility-scored applicants through a dedicated portal.
>
> Discovery stays free permanently. We never charge a founder to find money, and we never
> take a commission from a grant they win.

## Competition and why we win

> Nigerian founders currently rely on WhatsApp groups, Facebook pages and blog roundups.
> These are manual, unverified, frequently out of date, and list opportunities that
> closed months ago.
>
> Three differences. Our pipeline is **automated**, so listings stay live. Our matching is
> **personalised**, so founders are not reading a generic list. And we **help them apply**,
> which is where most drop out — finding the grant was never the hard part.

# PART 5 — ANSWERS THAT WORK FOR ANY OF THE THREE

## The group structure

> A2F Partners Limited is a Nigerian venture studio and capital-infrastructure company.
> It builds and operates companies in fragmented, under-digitised sectors, and builds the
> technology that makes those companies legible to institutional capital. FundFinder is
> its origination platform; OneUp Africa and FarmID are operating businesses in the
> portfolio.

## "Why should we fund you?"

> Because we do the unglamorous part. Most Nigerian businesses are not unfundable — they
> are unreadable: no clean books, no verified records, no structure an institution can
> price. We build the systems that fix that, and we have applied them to our own
> portfolio first. We are not proposing a theory. We are running it on ourselves.

## Risks and how we manage them

> **Data reliability** — funding sources publish inconsistently. We verify every listing
> and re-check live status rather than publishing on trust.
>
> **Regulatory** — we never hold client funds and never act as a lender, fund manager or
> broker. Regulated steps are executed by licensed operators.
>
> **Concentration** — a single-market dependency on Nigeria is mitigated by a roadmap
> into other West African markets using the same pipeline.
